CityVoice terms of use

Last updated: October 1, 2026

These terms apply when you use CityVoice through Alexa or through its web simulator. By using it, you accept them. They are short, and we tried to keep them plain.

CityVoice is not an emergency service

If anyone is in danger, call 911. CityVoice is for problems that can wait, like a pothole or a missed trash pickup. When something you say sounds like an emergency, CityVoice tells you to call 911 and files nothing, but it cannot call for help on your behalf.

This is a pilot

CityVoice was built for the Amazon Build, Ship, Shape hackathon. It runs on demonstration data for Washington DC and reads San Francisco's public 311 requests, and reports are not sent to any city: they go to a sandbox that imitates a city's 311 system. In Washington DC, request numbers, statuses and the reports of other residents are made up for the demonstration. To report a real problem, use DC 311 or SF311.

Using it fairly

Please report problems you have actually seen, and do not use CityVoice to harass anyone, to file reports in bulk, or to get around its limits. We may slow down or block use that puts the service at risk for others.

No guarantee

We do our best to keep CityVoice working and accurate, but it is provided as it is, without any warranty. It may be unavailable at times, and it may be changed or shut down when the pilot ends. To the extent the law allows, we are not liable for any loss that comes from using it.
